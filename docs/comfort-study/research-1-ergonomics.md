# Research report 1 — ergonomics literature, hands and wrists

FINDINGS

1. Carpal tunnel pressure rises with wrist extension and radial deviation; keep extension under 30 deg and radial deviation under 15 deg. Rempel et al. 2008, 20 typists: pressure 1.9 kPa at 0 deg, 2.3 at 15 deg ext, 3.1 at 30 deg, 4.0 at 45 deg; 3.6 kPa at 15 deg radial, 2.7 at 15 deg ulnar. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2649727/ Strong.

2. Posture guidelines from pressure thresholds, 37 subjects: at 30 mmHg the 25th-percentile angles were extension 32.7, flexion 48.6, radial 21.8, ulnar 14.5 deg; at 25 mmHg: 26.6, 37.7, 17.8, 12.1 deg. Ulnar deviation is the tightest limit. Keir, Bach, Hudes, Rempel 2007, Human Factors. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17315846/ Strong.

3. ANSI/HFES 100-2007 section 5.2.1.1 reference postures: elbow 70 to 135 deg, shoulder abduction under 20, shoulder flexion under 25, wrist flexion under 30, wrist extension under 30, torso-to-thigh 90 deg or more. Seated input-device support surface: height 56 to 72 cm, tilt range -15 to +20 deg. https://www.xybix.com/hubfs/ANSI_HFES_100-200727E2.pdf Strong (consensus standard).

4. Surface slope and height set wrist extension. Simoneau and Marklin 2001, 30 subjects: mean extension 22 deg at +15 deg slope, 9 deg at -15 deg slope (far edge lower); 21.8 deg with keyboard below elbow vs 7.3 deg above elbow. https://epublications.marquette.edu/mechengin_fac/4 Strong.

5. Forearm support at resting elbow height lowers shoulder and neck EMG; support above elbow height raises it above the no-support level. Zhu and Shin 2012, Ergonomics, 24 subjects. https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00140139.2012.709541 Moderate (abstract only).

6. Lap use vs desk: wrist extension +8 deg, head tilt +6 deg, shoulder flexion -13 deg, ulnar deviation -9 deg. A lap support vs bare lap: extension -9 deg, head tilt -4 deg, neck flexion -2 deg. All lap setups still showed high extension. Asundi et al. 2010, Ergonomics. https://www.researchgate.net/publication/41002099 Moderate (notebook keyboard, not a controller).

7. Button force. ANSI/HFES 100-2007 6.2.1.9: frequently used buttons 0.25 to 1.5 N, displacement 1.0 to 6.0 mm, must hold the resting finger weight; keys preferred 0.5 to 0.6 N, 2.0 to 4.0 mm. Strong. Gerard et al. 1999, 24 typists, 0.28/0.56/0.72/0.83 N: 0.83 N gave 36 to 40 percent more discomfort; 17 of 24 preferred 0.72 N with a click; users apply 3 to 7 times the make force. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10635542/ Moderate.

8. Sanwa OBSF-30 vendor spec: 55 gf (0.54 N) operating force, 0.85 mm pretravel, 2.53 mm total travel, 81 gf total. https://paradisearcadeshop.com/products/sanwa-obsf-30-snap-in-button-red Anecdotal (retailer sheet).

9. MIL-STD-1472F Figure 12 fingertip pushbuttons: diameter 10 to 25 mm, resistance 2.8 to 11 N single finger (1.4 to 5.6 N when several fingers act together), displacement 2 to 6 mm, edge separation 13 mm min, 50 mm preferred (6 mm min for simultaneous fingers). Hand joysticks: travel max 45 deg, 100 mm side and 50 mm rear clearance, "shall be mounted to provide forearm support". https://www.denix.osd.mil/soh/denix-files/sites/21/2016/03/02_MIL-STD-1472F-Human-Engineering.pdf Strong for the standard, but a vehicle and gloved-hand context.

10. Contact pressure: thenar, thumb-index web and pisiform are the most pressure-sensitive hand areas; female pain threshold about two-thirds of male. Fransson-Hall and Kilbom 1993, Applied Ergonomics 24(3). Secondary sources quote discomfort thresholds about 100 kPa thenar, 190 to 200 kPa fingers and palm, pain about 450 to 500 kPa, and 50 kPa as the sustained limit for blood flow. https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8123e60eabdc1d684977ed0325ba4bc1b99555b6 Moderate for regions, weak for kPa values. ANSI adds: palm supports under 3.8 cm deep raise contact pressure.

11. Esports injuries, 153 collegiate players: 26.8 percent injured in a year; wrist 33 percent of injuries; more than 5 h/day practice in 31.7 percent of injured vs 10.7 percent of uninjured (p=0.01); days per week not associated. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9749791/ Moderate.

12. Systematic review, 16 studies, 62,987 participants: more than 3 h/day gaming predicts musculoskeletal disorders, odds ratios 1.3 to 5.2, neck, shoulder and back most affected. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9288077/ Moderate.

13. Wrist extensor fatigue: 32 male esports players, two 90 to 120 min sessions; extensor EMG amplitude fell 15.8 to 16.6 percent, median frequency 3.3 to 3.7 percent; a 10 min passive break gave no recovery. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12400618/ Moderate (keyboard and mouse).

14. Microbreaks every 20 min reduced neck, back, wrist and shoulder discomfort more than every 40 min or ad lib. McLean et al. 2001. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11394463/ Moderate (small sample).

15. Low seat: at 60 percent of lower-leg length vs 100 percent, lumbar flexion 45.1 vs 29.6 deg and posterior pelvic tilt 24.2 vs 16.6 deg (20 subjects, stand-to-sit motion). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8752281/ Weak for sustained beanbag sitting.

16. Sanwa JLF lever: about 10 deg free play before engage, stock spring about 0.9 lb (4 N). https://www.slagcoin.com/joystick/restrictors.html Anecdotal.

DESIGN-RELEVANT THRESHOLDS

- Wrist extension: target 15 deg or less, hard max 30 deg. Flexion under 30.
- Ulnar deviation: target 10 deg or less, max 12 to 15 deg. Radial max 15 deg.
- Elbow 90 to 110 deg (within 70 to 135). Shoulder abduction under 20 deg, flexion under 25. Torso-thigh 90 deg or more, so hips above knees in the beanbag.
- Panel height: hand contact surface at, not above, resting elbow height. A lap places it below the elbow, which alone adds roughly 10 to 15 deg of extension (finding 4). Compensate with rake or a raised forearm rest, not by lifting the shoulders.
- Panel rake: far edge lower than near edge by the forearm's downward slope, roughly 7 to 15 deg, within the ANSI -15 to +20 deg envelope. Positive arcade-style rake (far edge higher) worsens extension on a lap.
- Forearm and palm support: full-width front rest at least 38 mm deep, rounded edge, no load on the pisiform or thenar; sustained pressure under 50 kPa, well under 100 kPa.
- Button force at make 0.5 to 0.8 N, never under 0.25 N, avoid 0.83 N and up; tactile click preferred. Total travel 2 to 4 mm. OBSF-30 at 0.54 N, 2.5 mm fits.
- Button edge gap: 30 mm buttons at 36 mm centres give about 6 mm, which meets only the MIL simultaneous-finger minimum; keep ergonomic layout arcs so fingers land without ulnar deviation.
- Lever: throw under 45 deg, 100 mm side and 50 mm rear clearance, forearm supported.
- Sessions: break every 20 to 30 min; passive 10 min breaks do not restore extensor capacity; daily play over 3 h raises injury odds.

CONTRADICTIONS AND GAPS

- MIL-STD-1472F wants 2.8 N minimum button force; ANSI/HFES and Gerard put the comfort ceiling near 0.8 N. Use ANSI for a bare-hand, high-frequency device.
- Cabinet panels rake up away from the player at standing elbow height; on a lap the surface is below the elbow, so the evidence points to the opposite slope. No study tests rake for a lever plus buttons.
- No peer-reviewed data on ball-top lever grip, fightstick posture, asymmetric left-lever right-button loading, thigh contact pressure from a controller, or sustained beanbag sitting.
- Lap and forearm-support findings come from keyboards; esports data are keyboard-and-mouse, male, 5 to 10 h/day.
- Kilbom kPa thresholds and the OBSF-30 and JLF numbers are unverified secondary or vendor figures.
- ANSI's 30 deg wrist limit is a ceiling; pressure is already elevated at 15 deg, so treat 15 deg as the design target.
- Zhu and Shin support heights in cm could not be retrieved.
