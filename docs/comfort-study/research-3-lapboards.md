Computed from the ANSUR II working database CSV (US Army 2012, male n=4082, female n=1986, mm; https://calmcode.io/static/data/ergonomics.csv, origin https://phc.amedd.army.mil/topics/workplacehealth/ergo/Pages/Anthropometric-Database.aspx). 5th / 50th / 95th:

| Lower thigh circumference (above knee) | 359 / 408 / 463 | 347 / 398 / 465 |
| Popliteal height | 390 / 430 / 471 | 350 / 387 / 428 |
| Buttock-popliteal length | 459 / 502 / 548 | 441 / 483 / 535 |

Cross-checks: FAA HFDS ch.14 (1988 Army data) thigh clearance M 149/168/190, F 140/158/180. Pheasant British adults thigh thickness M 130/155/180, F 120/150/180. Army sample is leaner than civilians; treat 95th values as low.

Derived (estimates, unverified): upper-thigh width per leg is about half of seated hip breadth, 167 to 228 mm across genders and percentiles; circumference/pi gives 168 to 230 mm, consistent. Width just above the knee is about 110 to 150 mm. Thigh gap at the crotch is ~0. No published data on the inter-thigh gap in relaxed sitting was found; 0 to 100 mm at mid-thigh is a plausible range. Thigh crest spacing (the two contact lines) is therefore ~170 to 230 mm with knees together, growing to ~250 to 350 mm with a 100 mm knee gap. A 280 mm wide device sits with both edges on or outside the crests only while crest spacing is under ~250 mm; beyond that its edges ride the inner slopes and it can rock.

## 3. Findings

1. Conforming bean/microbead bases get the best stability comments on soft seating because they fill the valley between the thighs and the sofa cushion curve (TechGearLab, Wirecutter on LapGear Designer). Confidence: medium, reviewer consensus, no measurements.
2. Flat rigid foam works only when the board is wide enough and heavy enough to load both thighs (K63 full-width memory foam "helps it to set level"). Without a centring feature it drifts: PCWorld missed the Lapdog's "slight bump ... which helped keep it centered between my legs". Medium.
3. Two-rail bolster bases are the most criticised: Huanuo dual cushion loads one leg, Mind Reader "not as stable ... narrow bolstered padding design" (TechGearLab). Rails that do not match the user's thigh spacing produce rocking. Medium.
4. Mass helps friction but hurts if off-centre: Lapdog 2.63 kg still seesaws; Sova praised because mass is centred and the user can slide the keyboard square over the thighs. Keep centre of mass low and between the thigh crests. Medium-high.
5. Front edge: a steep front drop-off is painful on the palms (Lapdog); a padded, angled front wrist rest is praised (Turret, Sova). The lap desk raises the work surface off the thighs: Asundi et al. measured head tilt down 4 deg, neck flexion down 2 deg, wrist extension down 9 deg versus bare lap (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/41002099). High for the study, medium for edge geometry.
6. Tilt: thighs slope down to the knee on a sofa, so items slide forward (Sova, K63, Huanuo). Bean-bag desks offer 0 to 20 deg (Posture-Rite); lapboards are 0 deg and rely on lips or friction. A few degrees of rearward tilt or a front lip compensates. Medium.
7. Bypassing the thighs: Couchmaster bridges 200 mm cushions beside the hips; older patents (US2763522, US2596094) use struts to the chair seat retained by the thighs. Both avoid thigh-slope instability entirely. Medium.
8. Heat: full-contact foam or bead bases trap heat (TechGearLab, K63 users); patent US12402720 (2025, single foam block lap desk) adds front-to-rear cooling channels and a U-shaped "hugging" recess. Low.

## 4. Gaps

- No measured tilt or rocking tests exist; all stability verdicts are subjective.
- No beanbag-chair-specific reviews found.
- No anthropometric data on knee or thigh gap in relaxed sitting.
- DINED (Dutch) needs a login; not used.
- Razer Turret Xbox dimensions unverified; K63 foam thickness conflicts between sources.
- Corsair official weight page has no spec; 1.85 kg is from reviews.
